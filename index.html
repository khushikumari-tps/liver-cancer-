Know the signs

Liver Cancer Symptoms

Early liver cancer often causes no symptoms. Recognising these signs can help you seek timely medical evaluation.

01

Unexplained Weight Loss

Losing weight without trying, over weeks or months, can be an early sign that should be checked.

02

Loss of Appetite & Nausea

Feeling full after small meals, reduced appetite, nausea or vomiting that does not settle.

03

Abdominal Pain

Pain, heaviness or fullness in the upper right abdomen, sometimes felt in the right shoulder or back.

04

Jaundice

Yellowing of the skin and eyes, dark urine, pale stools or itching caused by a build-up of bile.

05

Abdominal Swelling

A swollen abdomen from fluid build-up (ascites) or an enlarged liver that can be felt under the ribs.

06

Fatigue & Unexplained Fever

Persistent tiredness or weakness, and fever without an obvious cause, should be evaluated.

Seek Urgent Medical Attention

Sudden severe abdominal pain, rapidly increasing jaundice, vomiting blood or black stools, high fever with shivering, confusion or drowsiness require urgent medical assessment.

Understanding liver cancer

Types of Liver Cancer

Liver cancer is named after the cell it starts from, or the organ it has spread from. The type influences how it is assessed and treated.

Hepatocellular Carcinoma

The most common primary liver cancer. It starts in the liver cells themselves and often develops in a liver already affected by cirrhosis, hepatitis B or C, or fatty liver disease.

Discuss with our team

Intrahepatic Cholangiocarcinoma

A cancer that starts in the small bile ducts within the liver. It is less common than hepatocellular carcinoma and is assessed and treated differently.

Discuss with our team

Secondary / Metastatic Liver Tumours

Cancer that has spread to the liver from another organ, most often the bowel (colorectal liver metastases). Selected patients benefit from liver surgery as part of their cancer treatment.

The type, size and stage of a liver tumour can only be confirmed by imaging, blood tests and, where required, biopsy.

Patient education

What Is Liver Cancer?

Liver cancer is a growth of abnormal cells in the liver. It may start in the liver itself (primary liver cancer) or spread to the liver from another organ (secondary or metastatic liver cancer).

How it develops

The liver filters blood, processes nutrients and produces bile. Long-term damage from hepatitis B or C, alcohol, fatty liver disease or cirrhosis can cause liver cells to change and grow abnormally. Cancers of other organs, especially the bowel, can also spread to the liver through the bloodstream.

Where it appears

A tumour can develop in either lobe of the liver, close to the major blood vessels, or in the bile ducts that run through the liver. Its exact position and relationship to these vessels is what determines whether it can be removed safely.

Why symptoms come late

The liver has a large reserve, so small tumours rarely cause symptoms. Pain, jaundice, swelling and weight loss usually appear only when a tumour is larger or the liver is not working well — which is why people at risk are advised to have regular surveillance scans.

Why evaluation matters

The type, size, number and position of tumours, and how well the rest of the liver works, guide whether surgery, ablation, transplantation or medical therapy is appropriate. Careful staging avoids both under-treatment and unnecessary surgery.

Diagnosis and treatment should be based on imaging, blood tests and multidisciplinary review of your individual case.

Treatment

Liver Cancer Treatment

Treatment is matched to the type and stage of the cancer, liver function and your overall health.

Liver Resection

Surgical removal of the part of the liver containing the tumour, preserving enough healthy liver for normal function.

Ablation

Heat-based treatment (radiofrequency or microwave) that destroys small tumours through a needle, guided by imaging.

Liver Transplantation Evaluation

Assessment of whether a transplant is the right option for selected patients, particularly with cirrhosis, and guidance to the appropriate pathway.

Chemotherapy

Medicines given before or after surgery, or as the main treatment, depending on the type and stage of the cancer.

Targeted Therapy & Immunotherapy

Newer medicines that block cancer growth signals or help the immune system recognise and attack cancer cells.

Multidisciplinary Cancer Treatment

Surgeons, medical oncologists, radiologists and hepatologists review each case together to plan the right combination of treatments.

Liver surgery

Liver Resection — Hepatectomy

Liver resection removes the cancerous portion of the liver while preserving healthy liver tissue whenever appropriate. The remaining liver can regenerate over the following weeks. Which approach suits you is a clinical decision.

Approach 01

Open Liver Resection

The surgeon works through an incision under the right ribs. The tumour is removed together with a margin of healthy tissue, and the blood vessels and bile ducts supplying that segment are carefully controlled. Anything from a small wedge to a full lobe (hemi-hepatectomy) may be removed, depending on the tumour.

  • Direct access for large, multiple or centrally placed tumours and those close to major vessels.
  • Often considered for complex liver tumours, re-operations and combined bile duct or vascular procedures.
  • Liver volume and function are assessed before surgery to make sure enough healthy liver remains.
Approach 02

Minimally Invasive Liver Surgery

The resection is carried out through several small incisions using a camera and specialised instruments, either laparoscopically or with robotic assistance. Suitability depends on the size, number and position of the tumours, previous surgery and your fitness for anaesthesia.

  • Small incisions, with the operation viewed on a magnified screen.
  • Not suitable for every tumour or every patient; conversion to open surgery is sometimes needed for safety.
  • Robotic assistance may be considered for selected resections after clinical assessment.

The appropriate surgical approach is decided after imaging, liver function assessment and multidisciplinary review.

Hepato-Pancreato-Biliary

HPB Surgery

HPB stands for Hepato-Pancreato-Biliary surgery — operations on the liver, bile ducts, gallbladder and pancreas.

Liver Surgery

Resection of primary and secondary liver tumours, benign liver lesions and liver cysts, by open or minimally invasive approach.

Discuss with our team

Bile Duct Surgery

Repair and reconstruction of the bile ducts for strictures, injuries after gallbladder surgery, choledochal cysts and stones.

Discuss with our team

Gallbladder Cancer Surgery

Radical cholecystectomy — removal of the gallbladder with the adjacent liver tissue and lymph nodes — for gallbladder cancer.

Discuss with our team

Bile Duct Cancer Surgery

Surgery for cholangiocarcinoma of the bile ducts, which may involve removing part of the liver or pancreas together with the affected duct.

Discuss with our team

Pancreatic Surgery

Whipple procedure, distal pancreatectomy and other pancreatic resections for pancreatic cancer, cystic tumours and chronic pancreatitis.

Complex Hepatobiliary Procedures

Two-stage liver resections, vascular resection and reconstruction, and re-do surgery for recurrent or locally advanced tumours.
